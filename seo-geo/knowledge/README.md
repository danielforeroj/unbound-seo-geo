# Knowledge base: start here

What is known about ranking in search and being named or cited by AI answer
engines, and how strongly each claim is backed. Last verified 2026-10-30.
Sources are listed by name and URL in `../CREDITS.md`. Claims are in our own
words.

## Evidence grades

| grade | meaning | how to use it |
|---|---|---|
| A | Official: the search engine or AI company says it in its own docs | treat as rule |
| B | Study with a published sample and method, or a vendor dataset | treat as direction; quote the sample |
| C | Practitioners agree, or our own data | use, say it is experience |
| D | One creator | never quote as fact in a deliverable |
| X | Avoid, against policy or disproved | the never list in `risk.md` |

When two claims conflict, the higher grade wins. Studies from vendors that sell
a tool are B at best and can be skewed to their product. Studies age fast:
engines and models change every few weeks.

## Five beliefs that run the whole skill

1. GEO is mostly SEO. Google says its AI features use normal Search ranking and
   need no special markup (A). One program, not two.
2. Ranking is the entry ticket, not the seat. Overlap between top-10 results and
   AI citations is falling, but a page that is not indexed cannot be cited (B).
3. Each engine cites different sources. Plan and measure per engine.
4. What only the business knows is the strongest lever: original data, real
   cases, first-hand detail. Presence on other people's sites is the second.
5. Measure counts over many runs. No position inside an AI answer, no promised
   ranking.
