# Content: what to write and how

Conclusion: write what only the business knows, commercial pages first, one
page per real need, answer on top. AI can draft; a person owns the facts.
Grades: A official, B study, C practitioners or own data, D one creator.

## Write what only the business knows

- **Non-commodity content is the main lever (A).** Google contrasts generic
  advice with first-hand experience and says unique, useful content influences
  generative AI presence more than anything else it lists. Its helpful-content
  questions make a good rubric: original information, complete coverage,
  insight beyond the obvious, value over other results, not mass-produced.
- **The workflow (C).** Interview the owner or expert one question at a time,
  about 10 questions, about a real job or a real customer question. Write from
  the answers. Use the questions in `references/2-plan.md`.
- **Topical depth over breadth (C).** Answer every question a buyer asks about
  one topic, interlinked, before moving to the next topic.
- **Commercial intent first for B2B (C).** Service, comparison, alternatives and
  pricing pages face less competition and convert better. Informational posts
  are the part AI summaries replace most, so they earn a place only with the
  business's own data or as support for a money page.
- **Audience before keywords (B).** SparkToro found people phrase the same
  intent very differently. Write in the segment's language, taken from sales
  calls, reviews and support tickets, not only from one keyword tool.
- **Keyword sources.** Search Console queries filtered for question words and
  `best|top|vs|review|comparison|alternative`, People Also Ask, autocomplete,
  Reddit and Quora threads (`site:reddit.com "how do I" [topic]`), Keyword
  Planner. Google's advice: use your audience's words and do not chase every
  variation (A).

## Every page follows one structure (C, nothing Google contradicts)

1. Title, slug, H1 and first sentence say what the page answers, in the words
   people use. One main target per page. Descriptive slugs.
2. Direct answer in the first 40 to 100 words, or a 2 to 3 sentence summary on
   top.
3. Proof next: the business's numbers, a named expert, a case, a table.
4. Short sections under descriptive headings. Lists and HTML tables for data.
5. Specific numbers over vague claims.
6. Sourced statistics and quotes, linked where the claim is made (B, lab).
7. Visible author with a real bio page, published and updated dates. Never a
   fabricated author (A).
8. Internal links to and from the topic cluster.
9. Relevant images. Video on its own indexed watch page (A).
10. One clear call to action.

Google says you can ignore: word count targets, heading counts, meta keywords,
keywords in the domain, chunking for AI, rewriting just for AI (A). There is no
duplicate-content penalty, but copying others counts against you (A).

Meta description: it does not rank and may drive clicks, and Google often writes
its own snippet (A). Write one, do not agonize.

FAQs: visible, short answers to real questions are fine. Piles of generated
questions add nothing.

## Improve what already ranks first

- Queries around positions 3 to 20 with impressions: improve the title, add a
  section that uses the phrase.
- High impressions and very low click rate: rewrite title and description.
- A top page's query export plus the article, given to an LLM: ask for
  uncovered query clusters and missing headings.
- Refresh means real change: new data, tighter intro, new sections. Date
  changes do not work (A).

Steady beats volume. Many topics hoping some rank, and heavy automation, are
Google's warning signs (A). Plan 90 days before judging.

## AI drafts, a person owns the facts

- Google does not penalize AI use. It penalizes content made mainly to
  manipulate rankings (A).
- Google's page on AI-generated content says to manually fact-check and review
  all of it, and extends that to titles, meta descriptions, structured data and
  alt text (A).
- Pipeline: AI drafts from the brief, a person supplies the facts, angle and
  final edit, every claim and metadata field is checked, and no page ships
  without something only the business knows. Disclose AI use where a reader
  would wonder how it was made (A, recommended).
- Rewriting a finished body with an AI "GEO" tool lowered visibility in a lab
  (B). See `geo.md`.

## Country and language versions

Same copy across country versions makes versions compete even with hreflang
(D, one case). Treat markets as separate: prices, regulations and examples
should differ, not only vocabulary. If buyers research in English, an English
page may matter too (`geo.md`).

## Honest comparison page, skeleton

Title: "[Your category]: [A] vs [B] vs [You] for [buyer]". First paragraph: who
each option suits, in one sentence each, including where you are not the best
fit. Then a table (price range, best for, limits, source link for each cell),
then a short section per option, then how you tested. Never rank yourself first
by default.
