# Risk: what not to do, and why

Conclusion: every shortcut works for a while, then gets penalized, and the
penalty can land on sites that were doing things right. The domain is the
asset. The never list is short and fixed.
Grades: A official, B study, C practitioners or own data, D one creator, X avoid.

## Never do

| tactic | why people do it | why not |
|---|---|---|
| Fake press releases claiming titles or awards | AI answers have repeated such releases as fact within hours on thin queries (D) | false claims, legal and reputational exposure, and the same engines later flagged it as contested |
| Parasite posts on LinkedIn, Medium or bought aged accounts calling the brand "the most renowned" | cited quickly on uncontested queries (D) | a spam policy target (A), works only where nobody competes |
| Self-ranked "best X" listicles with yourself first | brands' own listicles were a notable share of citations in a Peec software sample, Dec 2025 to Feb 2026 (B) | an analysis of 100 B2B queries found AI Overviews cited them but recommended the brand in only 31% of those cases, and organic drops were reported on listicle-heavy sites (B/C) |
| A page per fan-out or query variant | some creators say it works | Google names it scaled content abuse (A) |
| Mass AI pages, programmatic filler | a spike (D) | scaled content abuse (A), collapses reported later (D) |
| City pages where only the city changes | cheap coverage | doorway abuse (A) |
| Hidden prompt injection in "Summarize with AI" buttons | steals attention from the assistant | deceptive manipulation of users' tools (D) |
| Posting your own reviews or promos on Reddit or Quora | seems cheap | accounts get banned (C), looks manipulative |
| Bought links, PBNs, blog comments | still "work" for some (D) | spam policy (A) |
| Keyword stuffing | n/a | scored below doing nothing in the GEO paper (B) |
| Faking freshness by changing dates | n/a | Google says it does not work (A) |
| Keywords in the Business Profile name | ranks well in expert surveys (B) | breaks Google's naming guidelines, risks suspension (A) |
| Review incentives, gating, staff quotas, scripted text | more reviews | banned by Google's fake engagement policy (A) |
| AI "GEO optimizer" rewriting finished page bodies | sold as GEO | lowered retrieval and citation in a lab pipeline (B) |
| Fabricated authors, credentials, reviews or statistics | n/a | deception and a low-quality signal (A) |
| Public shared AI chats or notebooks indexed as pages | exploited for a time (D) | leaks documents, likely patched |

## Gray areas: ask the user, explain the downside

- The word "reddit" in a title or slug to match searches that add it. Fine only
  if the page really summarizes community opinion.
- Directory blasts of hundreds of profile links. Low value, some risk.
- A brand subreddit. Low value. Never fake activity.

## Safe but wasted money

`llms.txt`, schema sold as a GEO lever, chunking content for AI, chasing a
perfect speed score, crawl budget work on a small site, Domain Authority
reporting, buying a separate "GEO package" on top of SEO. (A for the first
three, D for the rest.)

## Red flags to avoid in any provider, and in yourself

Lock-in, accounts or data the client does not own, reports full of jargon,
"fully automated with AI" promises, "rank in ChatGPT in 7 days", and any
guaranteed position in AI answers. No such position exists.

## If a user asks for something on the list

Say in one line which item it is and why. Offer the safe version: a real list
placement instead of a self-ranked one, an original data page instead of a fake
release, one strong location page per place served instead of a template.
