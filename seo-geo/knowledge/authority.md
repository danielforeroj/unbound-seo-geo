# Authority: links, mentions, reviews

Conclusion: AI engines repeat what other sites say about a brand. The work is
getting named, accurately, on pages they already quote. Real mentions count;
manufactured ones are the target of spam systems.
Grades: A official, B study, C practitioners or own data, D one creator.

## What Google says (A)

- Links are still used, and there is "much more to Google Search than just
  links". Mark untrusted and user-generated links nofollow. (SEO starter guide)
- Seeking inauthentic mentions across the web is less helpful than it looks,
  because ranking focuses on quality and other systems block spam. (AI
  optimization guide)
- E-E-A-T is not a single ranking factor. Systems reward signals of trust, which
  matters most on health and money topics.
- AI features can reflect what blogs, videos and forums say about a product.

## Third-party validation is what engines trust (B, C)

A University of Toronto preprint (1,000 consumer ranking prompts, 2025) found
AI search cites independent sites far more than Google's results do (B,
direction only, mid-2025 models). Practitioners agree: the engine cannot verify
your own claims, so it repeats others. Peec's most-cited domain lists are mostly
third parties (B vendor).

## Link and mention sources, ranked for use

| source | grade | use |
|---|---|---|
| Trade press covering your original data | C | core of authority work |
| Third-party "best X" lists, comparison sites, G2, Capterra, industry roundups | C | core for B2B |
| Podcasts and guest posts on relevant sites | C/D | yes, selectively |
| Sponsorships, events, partner pages, adjacent local businesses | D | yes, they bring more than links |
| Government, industry body, chamber of commerce listings | D | foundational |
| Business listings (Crunchbase, Product Hunt, Bing Places, Apple Maps) | D | hygiene for new sites |
| Mass directories, profile-link lists | D | low value, some risk |
| Blog comments, PBNs, bought links | X | never |

## How to do it

### 1. Find the targets

From `list_cited_sources` or your manual check, list every third-party page the
engines cite for your category questions. Sort by how often it appeared. Open
each. Is it a list, a review site, a press article, a video, a forum thread?
The type decides the approach.

### 2. Create something worth citing

One original piece: a survey, pricing data, a benchmark, results from your
clients (with permission). Publish it on your site as an original data page
with method and a stable URL (`references/page-types.md`). A single link from a
real niche publication is worth more than dozens of directory links (D, one creator).

### 3. Pitch the editors of the listed pages

Find the author or editor on the page. Send a short email. Template:

> Subject: [Data point] for your [list or article title]
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I read your piece "[title]". We have data it does not cover yet: [one
> sentence with the number and the sample, from your own data]. Method and
> numbers are here: [URL]. If it is useful for the next update, I can send the
> chart or a short quote.
>
> [Name, role, company]

Rules: reference their specific piece, give them something new, no mass
sending, no flattery filler, one follow-up after a week at most. Prepare the
email for the user to send. Never send it for them.

### 4. Ask to be added to a list honestly

If a "best X" list omits you and you fit, email the editor with facts, not a
demand: what you do, for whom, price range, a link to proof, and one reason
for inclusion. Do not offer payment for placement.

### 5. Claim and correct profiles

Review sites, Crunchbase, G2, Capterra, Business Profile and similar pages the
engines quote about you: claim them, fix the description, add the same facts
as your site. Wrong facts in an engine's answer usually trace to one of these.

### 6. Reclaim unlinked mentions

Search `"[brand name]" -site:yourdomain.com` and a news search. For each
unlinked mention, email the author a thank you and ask for a link to the
relevant page. Unlinked mentions may still help AI answers (D).

### 7. Reviews

Ask what engines quote: "what do people say about [brand]" in each engine, and
note which review sites appear. Follow each platform's rules: no incentives, no
gating. One creator saw an engine quote old poor reviews and improved results
after a normal review drive (D, anecdote). Local detail in `local.md`.

## Reddit and forums

Answer real questions in threads where you can help, disclose the affiliation,
never drop links to your own pages in every reply. A business posting for itself
gets banned (C). Do not run a brand subreddit to collect reviews.
