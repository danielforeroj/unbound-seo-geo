# Local SEO and local GEO

Conclusion: Google ranks local results on relevance, distance and prominence.
You cannot buy rank. The primary category, a page per service and genuine
reviews do the most. Each AI engine reads a different local source, so check
each one. Grades: A official, B study, C practitioners or own data, D one creator.

Caveats: Whitespark's factor list is an expert opinion survey, not measured
ranking data. BrightLocal sells listings and a local AI tracker.

## Google's rules (A)

- Local results rest mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. Prominence
  includes links to the business, review count and ratings. Money cannot buy a
  better local rank. (Business Profile help)
- The business name is the real-world name. No taglines, services or locations
  added. Keywords in the name help ranking in expert surveys and break the
  guidelines, which risks suspension. Never do it.
- One profile per location. Few, specific categories, describing what the
  business is.
- Service-area businesses hide the address and keep the area within about 2
  hours of driving. Virtual offices and mailboxes are not eligible.
- Reviews: no incentives, no gating (asking only happy customers), no staff
  quotas, no reviews with a conflict of interest. Asking every customer for a
  genuine review is allowed. (Google fake engagement policy)
- AI Overviews and AI Mode need no extra markup. Keep Business Profile and
  Merchant Center data current. LocalBusiness structured data supports booking
  and reservation features in AI Mode.

## What experts weight most (B, Whitespark 2026 survey, 47 experts)

- **Map pack:** primary category, proximity, address in the searched city, open
  at search time, high rating, address shown, additional categories, review
  count.
- **Local organic:** a dedicated page for each service, city or neighborhood
  relevance of content, quality of inbound links.
- **AI search visibility:** presence on expert "best of" lists, a page per
  service, prominence on industry domains, news and association mentions,
  authority of the review sites the business is on.
- **What costs ranking:** 1 and 2 star ratings, negative review sentiment, fake
  review reports, gating, too many reviews too fast, a service area beyond a
  2 hour drive.

Practice that follows:

- Name, address and phone consistency is hygiene. Keep it, do not sell it as a
  ranking lever (C).
- Reply to reviews for customers, not for ranking.
- Do not script what customers write.
- Service-area clients accept weaker map presence outside the base city and
  compete with city pages in local organic.

## City and service pages (A limit, C practice)

Google's spam policy names doorway abuse: sets of city pages that exist only to
catch searches. Practitioners agree city pages work when each carries unique
local content (projects, testimonials, landmarks, local questions) and fail
when only the city name changes. Rule: one page per service, one per city
really served, each with facts only that location has.

## Which source each AI engine reads (B vendor)

BrightLocal (Aug 2026, 60,970 checks, 1,355 locations in the US, UK and
Australia, AI Overviews, AI Mode and ChatGPT): Business Profile was the top
cited source overall, Yelp second, and Yelp dominated ChatGPT's local
citations. Gemini and Perplexity were not studied. Axios reported in July 2026
that ChatGPT will show Yelp reviews and details (C). BrightLocal's 2024 study
showed a different mix, so treat older data as outdated.

Plan that follows: Business Profile carries Google's AI and the map pack.
Yelp carries ChatGPT. Vertical directories (Angi, Thumbtack, Tripadvisor,
OpenTable as relevant) and local press carry the rest. Apple Maps and Bing
Places stay current for people.

## Customers and reviews (B, stated behavior)

BrightLocal's 2026 survey (1,002 US adults): 97% read reviews for local
businesses, 68% require at least 4 stars, 74% look for reviews from the last
three months, 47% will not use a business with under 20 reviews, and 45% use
generative AI for local recommendations, up from 6%. The direction is solid;
the size of the jump may reflect a changed question. So: a steady flow of
recent reviews, not a burst.

## Review request text (paste-ready)

Send to every customer after the job, same text, no screening:

> Hi [name], thanks for choosing [business]. If you have a minute, a review of
> how it went helps other people find us: [direct review link]. Honest
> feedback is welcome, good or bad.

Get the direct link in Business Profile: Get more reviews. Do not offer
discounts or gifts. Do not ask only satisfied customers. Do not write the
review for them.

## The working checklist

1. Business Profile complete: primary category, real extra categories,
   services, hours, photos, regular posts.
2. A page per service and per city served, with local facts, titles with
   service and city, internal links from a services hub.
3. Reviews as a flow from every customer, replies for customers.
4. Listings per engine: Business Profile (Google), Yelp (ChatGPT), the
   directories the engines quote for this category, Apple Maps, Bing Places.
5. Mentions AI reads: local "best of" lists, local press, associations. Email the
   editor with a factual pitch (`authority.md`).
6. A few real local links from adjacent businesses and partners.
7. LocalBusiness schema matching the page, with map and directions on the
   contact page.
8. A real about page: owner, licenses, awards, team.

## Local GEO check

In ChatGPT, Gemini or AI Mode and Perplexity, in the customer's language, ask
several times each: "best [service] in [city]", "[service] near
[neighborhood]", "is [brand] good". Record whether you appear, who appears
instead, and which sources each engine quotes. Log it in the format in
`references/manual-checks.md`. The gaps in each engine's source list are the
local work plan.

Never: keywords in the profile name, review incentives or gating, reviews the
business seeds on any site (`risk.md`).
