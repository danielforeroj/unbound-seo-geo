# Page types

What a plan can propose and what each type must carry. Commercial pages come
before informational content. Grades are explained in `knowledge/README.md`.

| type | when | must carry | never |
|---|---|---|---|
| Landing page | a campaign, an offer, a segment that converts | one need, one action, proof from the business | a landing page per keyword variant |
| Service page | each service genuinely sold | what it is, for whom, price logic or range, proof, FAQ from real questions | thin copy shared across services |
| Location page | each city or area really served | local facts, local proof, address rules for service-area businesses (`knowledge/local.md`) | city-swap templates where only the name changes |
| Comparison page | comparisons buyers actually make | a fair method, and places where you lose too | a "best X" list with yourself ranked first |
| Alternatives page | "alternatives to [competitor]" searches | who each option suits, honest limits, pricing where known | attacks, claims you cannot source |
| Original data page | you measure something nobody else does | a named benchmark, the result in the first third, visible method, a stable URL | numbers without method |
| FAQ and docs | recurring questions from sales and support | the real question as the heading, the answer first | questions invented to hold keywords |
| Blog cluster | a topic that supports a money page | your data or experience in each piece, links up to the money page | volume without an angle |
| Pillar page | a topic big enough to anchor a cluster | the sub-questions buyers ask, each as a heading, linked to detail pages | one page trying to rank for everything |
| About and author | always | the real owner, team, licenses, awards | fabricated people or credentials |

## What each page needs, in short

1. Title, slug, H1 and first sentence say what the page answers, in the words
   people use.
2. The direct answer in the first 40 to 100 words.
3. Proof next: your numbers, a named expert, a case, a table.
4. Short sections under descriptive headings. Lists and real HTML tables for
   data, never a table inside an image.
5. Sourced facts, linked where the claim is made.
6. Visible author, published date, updated date.
7. Internal links to and from the cluster.
8. One call to action.

## Example brief line

Need: "Does a dental clinic in Medellin do same-day crowns?" Type: service page.
Title: "Same-day dental crowns in Medellin | [Clinic]". First sentence: "Yes, we
make and fit a crown in one visit, in about two hours, for [price range]."
Proof: clinic's own case count and a dentist's name and license. Schema:
LocalBusiness plus FAQPage on visible questions only. (Illustrative: replace
every detail with the user's real facts.)
